## The process first, the tools second

Every automation starts from a simple map of the process as it is today: who does what, with which data, where time is lost. Often it is better to remove a step before automating it. Only then do I choose the tools, starting from the ones you already use: email, CRM, back-office systems, spreadsheets.

## Reliability above all

An automation has to behave well when something goes wrong. I plan for retries, checks that prevent duplicate operations, a log of every run and alerts when a person needs to step in. The result is a flow you can explain, not a black box.

## When AI is not needed

When the rules are clear, a traditional flow is simpler, cheaper and more predictable. I use AI where data is messy or text is free-form, for example to read a request and classify it, and I say so plainly when it adds nothing. If you want to know where to start, [let's talk](/contact).
